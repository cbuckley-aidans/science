Physical characteristic
Definition
A physical characteristic is any observable feature of an organism's body - something you can see.
Examples from the image: the zebra's stripes, the giraffe's long neck, the snake's lack of legs, the green tree frog's green skin.
Physical characteristics do not include behaviour (what an animal does) or habitat (where it lives). They describe the body.

What features could we use?
Look at the animals. On your whiteboard, write down physical characteristics that could split the group into a clear YES or NO.
A good characteristic is visible, gives a clear YES or NO, and splits the group rather than putting all animals on the same side.

Features that isolate one animal on their own are the most useful starting points for branching.

Step 2

Yes → Zebra  |  No → Step 3
Think aloudTwo remain: green tree frog and grey kangaroo. One more observable feature will separate them.
Step 3
Does it have green skin?
Yes → Green tree frog  |  No → Grey kangaroo
Done. Every animal reaches a unique endpoint. The key uses only observable physical characteristics.

Building a key.

Step 1
Does it have a very long neck?
Yes → Giraffe  |  No → Step 2
Think aloudGiraffe identified. Horse, lion, hare remain. Something stands out about the hare straight away.
Step 2
Does it have very long ears?
Yes → Hare  |  No → Step 3
Think aloudHorse and lion remain. I look at their feet - one has hooves, one has paws. That is a clear, observable split.
Step 3
Does it have hooves?
Yes → Horse  |  No → Lion
Notice: different animals need different characteristics. The logic is always the same - split, identify, repeat.
