How to Read Particle Diagrams

States of Matter Walkthrough

Step 1 of 6

What particle diagrams show

In these diagrams, each circle represents a single particle. To identify the state of matter, look at three things: how the particles are arranged, how far apart they are, and whether movement arrows are shown.

Solid

Regular grid-like pattern. Particles touching or almost touching. No movement arrows.

Liquid

Close together but no regular pattern. Short arrows may show particles sliding around.

Gas

Far apart with lots of empty space. Long arrows show particles moving fast in all directions.

Step 2 of 6

Identifying a solid

In a solid, particles sit in a regular, grid-like pattern packed tightly together. There are no movement arrows because particles cannot move from place to place — they only vibrate on the spot.

Regular pattern Tightly packed No movement arrows Vibrates in place

Step 3 of 6

Identifying a liquid

In a liquid, particles are close together but have no regular pattern — they are bunched up irregularly. Small arrows show the particles sliding past one another. The spacing is similar to a solid, but the arrangement is not fixed.

No regular pattern Still close together Short movement arrows Slides around

Step 4 of 6

Identifying a gas

In a gas, particles are spread far apart with lots of empty space between them. Long arrows show they move at high speed in all directions. The large gaps explain why gases have very low density and expand to fill any container.

Far apart Lots of empty space Long fast-movement arrows All directions

Step 5 of 6

Using written descriptions

Some cards give a written description instead of a diagram. Look for key phrases that match the properties of each state.

Solid clues

"regular pattern" · "fixed positions" · "vibrate" · "greatest density" · "least energy" · "strong attractive forces hold particles in place"

Liquid clues

"slide past" · "no fixed arrangement" · "can flow" · "takes the shape" · "close but irregular" · "cannot be compressed"

Gas clues

"far apart" · "high speed" · "fill any container" · "most energy" · "lowest density" · "forces too weak to hold them close"

Step 6 of 6

Energy and density

Solids have the most density (particles are most tightly packed) but the least energy. Gases have the least density but the most energy. These two properties always run in opposite directions across the three states.

If a card says "most dense" or "least energy" — that is a solid. If it says "most energy" or "least dense" — that is a gas. Liquid always sits in between.
