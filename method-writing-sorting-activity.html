Fix It — Walkthrough

Task: Separating Sugar from Sand

Step 1 of 7

Start with the experiment

Before writing a method, you need to understand what you are trying to do. The experiment sets out the goal and the key techniques you will use.

Experiment

"Separate a mixture of sugar and sand using water and filtration, then recover the sugar by evaporating the water."

This tells us we need steps for dissolving, filtering, and evaporating -- and that each step must be specific enough that any reader could carry it out independently.

Step 2 of 7

Read the weak method

Now look at what was written. Can you spot the problems that would make this impossible to follow reliably?

Weak Method

"We got some stuff and mixed it with water. Then we filtered it. After that we heated it up and got the sugar back."

First Person Too Vague Bundles Actions

"We got some stuff" tells a reader nothing about what equipment to use or how much of anything to measure. "Then we filtered it" skips the setup entirely. A good method must be reproducible by someone who has never done the experiment.

Step 3 of 7

Apply the four-point checklist

Run the weak method through each check to see exactly where it breaks down.

Third Person?

Is "I" or "we" avoided throughout?

✗ No. "We got", "we filtered", "we heated" are all first person.

Specific?

Are quantities, sizes, and equipment named?

✗ No. "Some stuff", "water", and "heated it up" contain no measurements.

Numbered & Ordered?

Are steps numbered and in the correct sequence?

✗ No. There are no numbers and the sequence is collapsed into vague phrases.

One Clear Action?

Does each step contain a single action?

✗ No. Multiple actions are bundled into each sentence.

All four checks fail. We need to rewrite the method so it passes every one.

Step 4 of 7

Fix the dissolving steps

"Got some stuff and mixed it with water" needs to become two separate, specific, numbered steps written in third person.

Specific

What equipment and quantities are needed?

A 250 mL beaker half-filled with 100 mL of water.

Fix

Rewrite as two third-person steps, one action each

1. A 250 mL beaker is half-filled with 100 mL of water.
2. One teaspoon of the sugar and sand mixture is added and stirred with a glass rod for 30 seconds until the sugar dissolves.

Step 5 of 7

Fix the filtering steps

"Then we filtered it" skips critical setup. The reader needs to know exactly how to prepare the filter before any liquid is poured.

Ordered

What has to be set up before filtering can begin?

Filter paper must be folded and placed in a funnel, which is positioned over a clean conical flask.

Fix

Rewrite as two ordered third-person steps

3. A piece of filter paper is folded and placed into a funnel positioned over a clean 250 mL conical flask.
4. The mixture is slowly poured through the filter paper.

Step 6 of 7

Fix the evaporation steps

"Heated it up" needs to be broken into specific steps that describe the equipment, the setup, and the safe shutdown in sequence.

One Action

What are the individual actions in this stage?

Transfer filtrate to evaporating dish, set up on gauze mat and tripod, light Bunsen burner, heat until water evaporates, turn off and allow to cool.

Fix

Rewrite as separate numbered steps

5. The filtrate is poured into an evaporating dish placed on a gauze mat on a tripod.
6. The Bunsen burner is lit and adjusted to a blue flame. The solution is heated until most of the water has evaporated.
7. The Bunsen burner is turned off and the evaporating dish is allowed to cool.

Step 7 of 7

The improved method

Compare the two. The improved method is numbered, specific, written in third person, and contains one action per step. Anyone could pick it up and repeat the experiment exactly.

Original (Weak)

"We got some stuff and mixed it with water. Then we filtered it. After that we heated it up and got the sugar back."

✓  Improved Method

1. A 250 mL beaker is half-filled with 100 mL of water. 2. One teaspoon of the sugar and sand mixture is added and stirred with a glass rod for 30 seconds until the sugar dissolves. 3. A piece of filter paper is folded and placed into a funnel positioned over a clean 250 mL conical flask. 4. The mixture is slowly poured through the filter paper. 5. The filtrate is poured into an evaporating dish placed on a gauze mat on a tripod. 6. The Bunsen burner is lit and adjusted to a blue flame. The solution is heated until most of the water has evaporated. 7. The Bunsen burner is turned off and the evaporating dish is allowed to cool. Sugar crystals remain in the dish.

Notice how every step has a number, a piece of named equipment, and one action. There is no "I" or "we", no vague words like "some" or "a bit", and no step that tries to do two things at once.
